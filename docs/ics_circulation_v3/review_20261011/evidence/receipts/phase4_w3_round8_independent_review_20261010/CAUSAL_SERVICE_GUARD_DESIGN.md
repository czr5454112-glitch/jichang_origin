## Why a same-future oracle is not an online guard

Consider a public prefix with one available tray at A. An optional empty dispatch removes it until time r. Two future streams can have exactly the same observations and forecast publications at decision time: in one, an unannounced task reaches A before r and the baseline parked tray serves it immediately; in the other, there is no such task and the dispatch helps a task elsewhere. An online controller sees the same prefix in both worlds and cannot choose differently by consulting the later outcome. Where no alternative timely tray exists, dispatch delays the first world's task. This is a direct indistinguishable-prefix construction, not a theorem imported from the literature and not a claim that every nontrivial improvement is impossible.

Consequently, empirical `C_new(i, future) <= C_base(i, future)` on sampled tapes is a useful offline diagnostic but cannot be used as a runtime oracle. An uncertainty set containing only nominal tasks read from the hidden event queue would also leak information: it quietly proves there are no other future arrivals. Published forecasts must be distinguished from an authoritative arrival envelope. Absence of a forecast is not an operational guarantee of absence.

The same limitation applies to a fallback: after a changed commitment, running the baseline on the changed state is executable, but it does not recover the baseline's counterfactual tray positions and past reservations. “Fallback exists” is weaker than “baseline service is preserved.” A future shadow baseline may consume newly observed arrivals when they actually occur; it must never read the physical engine's unreleased event tape.

## An executable staged design

**First separate existing commitments from speculative improvement.** Keep the original validator, capacity semantics, module parameters, FIFO loading, permissions, entered legs, and irrevocable protected/empty commitments. Represent any additional service promise outside `Bag.protected` as a record containing bag identity, reference timestamp, selected legal reference witness, completion bound, resource-owner validation version, and explicit coverage assumptions. There must be no optimistic promise if its witness is unavailable or budget-censored.

**For already loaded bags, a complete legal witness can provide a concrete bound.** A new opt-in central coordinator can request the existing owner's `whole_route` transaction for an ordinary bag without changing its business flag. The finite witness must satisfy the chosen per-bag service bound, preserve existing commitments, and remain actually reserved through unloading/hold. Original `ExecutionValidator` support exists, but using it for more ordinary tasks is a new control commitment contract and must be compared against the original `next_edge` policy. It is not a claim that the current continuation already does this. Promise preservation follows only under the existing deterministic travel/module assumptions and future actions respecting the same owner reservations; faults or capacity changes require their own disturbance model.

The reference bound must be stated honestly. A publicly computed baseline witness at time t supports “no later than this committed promise,” not “no later than whatever the unconstrained baseline would eventually achieve in the true future.” The latter remains the stronger paired evaluation target. For a task that is already late, a finite achievable promise can prevent further avoidable lateness; inventing an infeasible deadline promise cannot.

**Waiting or unannounced tasks need a separate certificate.** A waiting bag has not necessarily acquired a real tray. The current loaded-route transaction cannot reserve a fictitious loaded tray or override FIFO. A scratch matching count, shortest-path upper bound, or full-witness validation for some other bag is not an end-to-end service reservation for that waiting bag. Within unchanged APIs, a conservative fallback is to reject optional empty commitments whenever they consume the only exact stock or owned capacity needed by a visible critical queue, and let the original legal reactive/admission mechanism serve it. This is a rejection rule, not a blanket deadline guarantee. A stronger queued-service promise requires an explicit legal owner-supported admission/lease interface; it must be treated as a separate research change rather than smuggled into the guard.

**Use declared arrival intervals when they exist, not a hidden nominal tape.** For the publicly modeled jitter{-2,0,2}, retain the worst-case earliest allowed demand even if cancellation is possible. A source-stock check at forecast90 alone misses an allowed arrival88. A guard using an authoritative [88,92] envelope would reject the specific error07 depletion if no other tray can be at134 by88. This blocks a defined counterexample within that envelope; it does not cover unpublished tasks or certify joint path capacity. Any finite uncertainty set must record its source, publication time, identity/count rules, and residual “outside the set” risk. The current Round8 synthetic law is an evaluation law, not evidence that operations obey this bound.

**Keep the downstream continuation inside the protection scope.** Protecting only an immediate unload or empty move is insufficient. The released tray's later empty routing and the learned selector's later loaded choice can consume the benefit or delay another bag. For a promised loaded task, each later selection must retain a feasible reserved suffix within its existing bound; a new proposal may replace that suffix only through a legal atomic transaction that preserves the bound. A learned ranker can propose among certified actions, but cannot turn an uncertain proof into acceptance. Budget exhaustion returns unknown and uses the already retained legal continuation; it does not announce infeasibility or relax the promise.

These changes may sacrifice speculative gains. Measure that opportunity cost explicitly; do not silently tune away rejection or weaken the per-bag service target. The already proven terminal-capacity screen offers a separate type of improvement: less computation with identical physical choices under its stated query contract, avoiding service tradeoffs entirely.

## Necessary next validation, without new runs in this note

1. Keep both original late-service episodes and both Round6 later-but-on-time episodes as mandatory mechanism regressions. At each first intervention, require the same observed state, cursor, public forecasts, candidate domain, and exact baseline continuation. Preserve all later changed decisions; do not attribute the full episode effect to one edge without an isolation experiment.
2. Construct paired indistinguishable public prefixes with an unannounced future task, plus the early-boundary case arrival=forecast−2. A legitimate guard must make the same decision before the prefixes diverge. A hidden-tape mutation must not change the online decision.
3. Check full reservation coverage, half-open edge/node/reception windows, hold/unload times, no-wait nodes, old transit, FIFO tray identity, simultaneous admissions, and rollback of failed atomic transactions. Two individually feasible witnesses need not be jointly feasible.
4. Separate guarantee denominators: admitted promises, visible waiting tasks without promises, protected but unadmitted tasks, published uncertain demand, and not-yet-public arrivals. No category disappears from the final task ledger. Report promise violations, deadline regressions, any completion delay, J components, acceptance/rejection/unknown counts, and computational work together.
5. Retain the full original 26-policy×36-future paired design where applicable, and predeclare any new synthetic uncertainty family before results. A no-later subset selected from known outcomes is an oracle upper bound, not a deployable-policy evaluation. Holdout selection or expected-cost confidence cannot replace the per-task safety test.

## Literature checked and its actual boundary

Wabersich and Zeilinger's predictive safety filter constructs a feasible backup to a terminal safe set and preserves a usable backup when replanning fails. Its nominal and uncertain formulations have explicit model/terminal-set assumptions; this motivates retaining an executable service witness rather than merely checking one optimistic rollout. It does not establish the ICS per-bag baseline-dominance claim. Checked §4.1, Assumption4.2, and the backup/shrinking-horizon argument. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.05506v4).

Carr et al. describe belief-support shields under partial observability: allowed actions stay in a winning region for the model's support; the partial model must preserve or overapproximate possible transitions. Reach-avoid results additionally distinguish fairness from pure avoidance. This supports making the uncertainty support and liveness assumptions explicit. It does not license deleting unknown arrivals or treating a finite sample as complete support. Checked §3.1–3.3 and Theorems2–3. [AAAI primary paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/26723/26495).

SPIBB's Theorem2 is an approximate policy-performance guarantee with high probability under its batch-MDP assumptions. It concerns return relative to a baseline, not universal realized-future completion-time dominance of every bag. It can inform conservative learning research after coverage improves, but cannot substitute for an admission/commitment certificate here. Checked §2.2–2.3 and Theorem2. [ICML primary paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/laroche19a/laroche19a.pdf).
